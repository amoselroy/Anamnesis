WORLD PATTERN 2026-07-02 — Atomic operations on staleness markers prevent racing reclamation — 2026-07-04

PRINCIPLE: When reclaiming abandoned resources based on staleness timestamps, atomic filesystem operations prevent two racers from both believing they acquired the resource.

NARRATIVE: The worker lock needed a staleness-reclaim path: if a background worker crashes without releasing its lock, something must eventually clear that stale lock and allow the next worker to proceed. Initial implementation used a simple unlink-and-check: if `mtime` is older than threshold, delete it. But this had a TOCTOU window: two workers could both decide the lock is stale, both unlink it, and both proceed believing they held it. The fix was atomic rename: claim the old lock's parent directory entry via `rename(stale_lock, stale_lock + ".old")`, which exactly one racer can win. The racer that wins the rename holds the lock; the other loses cleanly and backs off. No further synchronization needed because filesystem renames are atomic. This pattern generalizes: whenever managing shared resources with periodic cleanup, the cleanup operation itself must be atomic at the point of transition (e.g., marking as claimed/reclaimed), not just idempotent.
