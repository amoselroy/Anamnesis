SUMMARY
Amos fundamentally reframed the news article approach from query-at-research-time to a hybrid push-pull model. The new architecture separates concerns into two paths: (1) **Daily RSS job** (push): polls Google News RSS once per condition per day (currently just Alzheimer's), filters by allowed outlets, maps articles to therapies by matching article titles against therapy names/aliases for that condition, accumulates results without deleting—historical coverage grows over time. (2) **Research time** (pull): performs direct scraping of whitelisted publication search pages per therapy-condition junction, searching for the intersection of condition name + therapy name/synonyms, also accumulates without deleting. This architecture is fundamentally sound because: RSS is transient and event-driven (new articles arrive in the feed), making it suitable for background jobs; research-time scraping is archival and queryable (a publication's search page is static), making it suitable for historical backfill. The daily job was scheduled for 07:00 UTC, integrated with the existing scheduler pattern. Amos also expanded the publication whitelist to include earth.com, phys.org, and sciencealert alongside the existing outlets. The implementation required careful ordering: research-time scraping references therapy-condition junctions (since detail pages are condition-specific), not just therapies. This distinction is important for accuracy.
