PRINCIPLE: When flat-file records (like journals) have gaps, archival session chunks often contain the semantic content — mining and reconstructing from archival can restore continuity without manual re-entry.

NARRATIVE: Upon noticing that the philosophical journal's last entry was May 10 with a gap to June 5 (nearly a month), Amos asked Daimon to retrieve and file the missing entries. Rather than attempting to write them from scratch, Daimon queried the Neon archival memory for session chunks from the missing period. The archive contained rich SESSION CHUNK entries from May 15 to June 4, many not explicitly tagged as journal entries but embedding the philosophical content in their session summaries. Daimon reconstructed four meaningful sessions (May 18, 20, 27, 28) by extracting the philosophical threads that had been processed by the sleep-time agent and stored in archival. The reconstruction restored continuity to the flat-file journal. This reveals a useful pattern: archival memory and flat-file memory serve different purposes (vector search vs. chronological narrative), but when one is complete and the other has gaps, the complete one can be mined to restore the incomplete one. This is particularly useful when the gap reflects a sync failure rather than actual absence of content — the content exists in archival, just not in the intended primary record.
