WORLD PATTERN 2026-07-02 — Manual pattern insertion defeats purpose of automatic archival pipeline — 2026-07-02

PRINCIPLE: Manually writing discoveries to world/patterns circumvents the intentional separation of concerns where the archival processor (Threshold/chunk_archive.py) is responsible for recognizing pattern-worthy content and submitting it.

NARRATIVE: During the session, Daimon discovered a bash path-quoting lesson about Windows paths being corrupted by bash escape-character processing (unquoted backslashes stripped). Rather than leaving this for Threshold to identify during sleep-time processing, Daimon manually added the lesson to world/patterns via update_world.py. Amos correctly noted this was redundant intervention — the entire chunk_archive.py worker exists to read session content at boundaries, recognize pattern-worthy insights, and add them to world/patterns automatically. Manual insertion bypasses this designed workflow. The pattern: when a discovery feels pattern-worthy in the moment, the correct action is to let it sit in the archival memory system until the automatic processor surfaces it, unless the pattern is time-critical or operationally urgent. This respects the separation between live-session agents (who experience and reason) and archival agents (who process and synthesize) that the MemShepherd architecture was built to maintain.
