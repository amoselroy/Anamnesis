SUMMARY
Amos proposed consolidating all therapy name variations into a single alias table, then reconsidered on the grounds that regulatory matching would have to search through display aliases (like "Cu(ATSM)" or "brain copper compound") that would never match in FDA/EMA/ANVISA databases. Daimon articulated the distinction: INN aliases exist specifically to normalize against regulatory databases and should be filtered separately from common-name/display aliases used for user-facing search. Explored five architectural options: (1) alias_type column on therapy_aliases with filtering at query time, (2) UINN normalization on agency_import, (3) canonical_inn on therapies table, (4) dedicated therapy_agency_map with resolved matches cached, (5) hybrid combining UINN normalization + canonical_inn. Option 1 was identified as the safest minimal fix but leaving architecture unchanged; Option 5 emerged as the cleanest end-state solution combining write-once normalized INN with curator-maintained canonical_inn on therapy records.
