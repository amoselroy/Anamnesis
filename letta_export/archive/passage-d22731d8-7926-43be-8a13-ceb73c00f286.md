SUMMARY
Amos requested a two-step interface for Manage News: step one selects the condition from available active conditions, step two displays the news articles for that condition with management options (Fetch, Score Sentiment, Hide, Include). This triggered investigation into the data model for condition-scoped news visibility. During implementation planning, a fundamental architectural gap was discovered: news articles were stored at the therapy level (each source row belonged to one therapy), but curation and visibility needed to be therapy-condition-junction-specific. An article about a drug for Parkinson's treatment should be removable from the Alzheimer's view of that therapy while remaining available and attachable to a Parkinson's view. The initial implementation added a `condition_id` foreign key to sources, attempting to scope each article to a specific therapy-condition pair, but this would result in duplicate rows when the same article was relevant to multiple conditions of the same therapy — inefficient and not semantically correct. The modal design itself was sound and implemented successfully (two-step flow with breadcrumb navigation), but the underlying schema needed rethinking to properly support condition-scoped visibility without duplication.
