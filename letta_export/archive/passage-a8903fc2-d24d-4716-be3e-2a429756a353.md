SUMMARY
As implementation progressed, Render's free tier limitations became increasingly central to architecture decisions. The platform's defining constraint is the 15-minute automatic sleep: any HTTP request within 15 minutes keeps the dyno awake, but scheduled jobs firing after 15 minutes of inactivity never execute because the process is already terminated. This forced synchronous retry logic with aggressive timing rather than distributed retries over hours. The 512MB RAM limit creates secondary pressure: full headless Chromium with system browser deps weighs 200-350MB at launch, leaving minimal headroom for FastAPI (~120MB) and Python runtime. Complex monthly monitoring job running all four agencies risks OOM kills if memory isn't carefully managed. The tension is between desiring comprehensive regulatory monitoring (EMA is richest dataset with explicit withdrawal/revocation tracking) and the practical constraints of free infrastructure. User's plan to upgrade to paid tier post-fundraising was acknowledged as the medium-term solution, but interim workarounds discussed: running EMA download as local process (outside Render), then uploading the file for import; or temporarily skipping EMA and accepting incomplete European data. User's observation about earlier successful EMA downloads suggests the problem may be intermittent rather than permanent, opening possibility that the URL is still functional and the 404s are transient infrastructure issues rather than broken paths.
