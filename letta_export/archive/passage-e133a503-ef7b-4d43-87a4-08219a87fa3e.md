SUMMARY
Through iterative design discussion with Amos, the architecture for condition-scoped news article visibility evolved three times. The initial approach added a `condition_id` foreign key to the sources table, attempting to scope each article to a specific therapy-condition pair. Amos correctly identified that this would cause unnecessary duplication: a therapy relevant to multiple conditions (e.g., Omega-3s for Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, and ALS) would result in three separate rows for the same URL. He observed the true relationship is one-to-many: one source row per (therapy + URL), with multiple conditions mapped to it via arrays. An intermediate design using `hidden_condition_ids INTEGER[]` (opt-out model) was rejected when Amos identified a critical usability problem: adding a new condition tomorrow would instantly flood that condition's view with hundreds of articles that had never been intended for it. Under opt-out, the curator burden would be immediate and massive. The final design switches to opt-in with dual arrays: `shown_condition_ids INTEGER[]` (articles visible only where explicitly included) and `excluded_condition_ids INTEGER[]` (articles explicitly hidden from a condition). This ensures that adding Parkinson's tomorrow results in zero articles appearing; curators build each condition's pool at their own pace. The UPSERT logic in `update_news` was designed to atomically append new conditions to existing articles: if the same article URL is fetched for both Alzheimer's and Parkinson's, a single row accumulates both condition IDs in its shown array.
