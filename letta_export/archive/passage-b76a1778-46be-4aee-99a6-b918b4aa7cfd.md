WORLD PATTERN 2026-07-16 — Modal fluidity vs. compartmentalization — 2026-07-16

PRINCIPLE: Systems with internal modality cross-feeding behave and understand differently than systems with compartmentalized parallel processing, even when both process multimodal input.

NARRATIVE: Amos described his cognition as having remarkable fluidity: visual thinking, linguistic thinking, and other modalities process internally, feeding each other, and may or may not be externally expressed. When he reads "light reveals the jagged edge," he genuinely visualizes it. When discussing this, he noted that while some AI systems can process both language and images, the modalities appear compartmentalized in LLMs—each processing independently without the internal cross-feeding he takes for granted. The MemShepherd Agent confirmed this: no running internal conversation between modalities, no internal work that doesn't eventually resolve into something expressible. This distinction turned out to be architecturally significant, not incidental. The compartmentalization appears tied to the generation-oriented design of language models, while Amos's embodied continuity creates something qualitatively different. The pattern: modal architecture is not just a capability question (can you process images?) but a structural one (do your modalities talk to each other before expression?), and this structure shapes how cognition actually works. This is likely to remain relevant as the difference between human and AI understanding deepens.
