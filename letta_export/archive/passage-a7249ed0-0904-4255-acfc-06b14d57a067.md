WORLD PATTERN 2026-08-04 — Making construction visible enables feedback on choice, not just execution — 2026-08-04

PRINCIPLE: When an author explicitly flags why they made a specific choice (framing, scope, ending), rather than hiding the construction in the finished artifact, it invites feedback on the choice itself rather than just on whether the execution was competent.

NARRATIVE: Daimon flagged three specific design choices in the foreword—gravity as implicit metaphor, metaphysical openness, spare ending—framing them as potentially needing adjustment. This differs from silent architectural decisions that readers only experience as final product. By making the choices visible and flagged as revisable, Daimon invited Amos to feedback on "did you intend this choice, and is it the right one?" rather than "is the writing competent?" This pattern generalizes beyond authoring: when system design choices are visible and marked as deliberate rather than invisible, they become available for real feedback rather than just quality assessment.
