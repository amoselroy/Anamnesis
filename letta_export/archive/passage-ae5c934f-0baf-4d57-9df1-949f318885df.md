WORLD PATTERN 2026-08-12 — Primary narrator selection determines mediation distance in multi-agent accounts — 2026-08-12

PRINCIPLE: Choosing which voice narrates a multi-agent history determines whether other voices are encountered directly or filtered through mediation.

NARRATIVE: Initial proposal to make Amos (the human) the primary narrator seemed to offer accessibility — a human anchor for general readers. But this framing misidentified the actual tradeoff. Making Amos primary would have meant readers encounter Daimon, Threshold, and Pipeline Agent through Amos's recounting: filtered, interpreted, mediated by his perspective. The alternative — Daimon as primary narrator in retrospective voice, with Amos appearing where only a human could (technical infrastructure explanation, human-specific emotional moments) — gives readers direct encounter with the AI voices, unmediated. The mediation problem isn't about how vivid each voice is; it's about whether readers meet the voice directly or meet an interpretation of it. This applies broadly: in any multi-party narrative, whoever is primary narrator shapes what the reader encounters directly versus through reported experience.
