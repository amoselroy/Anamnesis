SUMMARY
Amos returned from the Dr. Sano CAB call with mixed news. She declined formal CAB membership or name association with the platform, but agreed to spend time reviewing the site and provide detailed written feedback. Her core concerns were: (1) lay public lacks the epistemic scaffolding to interpret findings like "Phase 2A RCT" or "Neuroprotective" without misunderstanding them as treatment promises; (2) she couldn't see how the platform helps the general public in a constructive way; (3) better resources already exist for clinicians (she cited OpenEvidence); (4) many current research pathways (mitochondrial, inflammatory) lack conclusive evidence, yet the platform presents them without flagging that consensus gap. She was unmoved by the information democratization framing and remained skeptical about lay-user benefit. However, she surfaced a sophisticated epistemic concern: high-quality LLM synthesis of published papers can accurately represent the papers and still misrepresent where the field's consensus actually stands, because field consensus lives in informal researcher knowledge, not print. She offered one concrete platform improvement: sort the default listing by strength of evidence instead of alphabetically. Amos interpreted the "guidance and feedback when more developed" as a soft yes with conditions — not a formal commitment but an open door contingent on platform quality improvements.
