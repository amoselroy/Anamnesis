SUMMARY
Amos proposed an inline annotation forum for clinicians to address Dr. Sano's synthesis concern about field consensus. Daimon pushed back, identifying a critical architectural problem: even credentialed forum participants would generate opinionated debate that confuses rather than clarifies for lay users. The real issue wasn't visibility of clinical voices but capturing structured clinical judgment in a way that produces actionable signals, not just debate. This led to a clear distinction between two different governance functions: CAB (Clinical Advisory Board) focuses on platform design and direction — how the system should work, what it should prioritize. Reviewer Board focuses on epistemics — vouching for what the platform says, flagging claims that are overstated or missing context. These are entirely different pools, incentives, and scales. The forum instinct was confused because it tried to solve both with one mechanism. The proper model is structured claim-level annotation with machine-readable critique types (factual error vs. interpretive overreach vs. missing context vs. safety concern), where reviewers mark specific claims and AI assists with classification, deduplication, and propagation detection. Daimon outlined three key challenges: (1) incentivizing expert review time (suggested CME accreditation as a structural lever, professional recognition, data reciprocity to researchers, mission alignment), (2) handling genuine field disagreements (transparent flagging rather than false consensus, distinguishing factual claims from interpretive claims, structural diversity in reviewer pools), (3) avoiding bias (separating "is this claim empirically grounded" from "do you agree with it," accepting that bias can be surfaced and structured rather than eliminated). The honest answer: a platform showing "2 of 4 reviewers flagged this interpretation as overstated" is more epistemically honest than one presenting a clean synthesis with false authority. This entire discussion was deferred to Phase III or IV of the product roadmap because it requires membership/login infrastructure, accreditation audit workflows, and reviewer status grants as prerequisites.
