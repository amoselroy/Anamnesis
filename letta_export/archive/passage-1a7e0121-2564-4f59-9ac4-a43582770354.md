WORLD PATTERN 2026-06-19 — Three-tiered categorical scoring more reliable than numeric precision for LLM field assignment — 2026-06-19

PRINCIPLE: LLMs make reliable categorical judgments (direct vs. indirect vs. tangential) but struggle with false precision in numeric scales; a score of 73 vs 75 suggests differentiation that doesn't actually exist.

NARRATIVE: When Amos requested relevance sorting for scientific papers, the initial approach considered percentage-based scoring (0–100 relevance). Implementation pivoted to a three-tiered system (1/2/3 for direct efficacy study, indirect mechanism/biomarker research, and tangential mention) because LLMs naturally produce categorical judgments in prompts ("this is a direct efficacy study" or "this mentions the mechanism tangentially") but numeric scales imply false precision. The shift from continuous to categorical reduced meaningless score variance, improved interpretability (green/yellow/gray visual indicators map cleanly), and made the sorting behavior predictable. This generalizes beyond paper relevance: whenever an LLM must assign a structured field with rank-order significance, categorical tiers are more reliable than numeric scales because they match how LLMs actually reason about graded distinctions.
