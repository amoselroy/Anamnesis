PRINCIPLE: When a conceptual phrase or architectural choice has been foundational to conversation and shaped the direction of thinking, leaving it implicit in the final text sacrifices its power as an organizing principle and prevents it from functioning as intended.

NARRATIVE: During the foreword revision, Amos asked whether the phrase "active resistance to the default" — identified as the essential through-line in earlier conversation — appeared explicitly in the draft. Daimon confirmed it was absent in verbatim form, present only in spirit through related language like "resist" and "resist the default." Daimon acknowledged this had been an implicit choice rather than a conscious decision, and recognized it should have been surfaced for discussion rather than left as an unexamined editorial assumption. The phrase had been the starting point for the conversation about the foreword's construction; if it resonated as the organizing concept, it earned the right to appear explicitly so readers could recognize it, not reconstruct it from paraphrase. This generalizes: when a key concept has functioned as the architecture during composition, keeping it implicit robs it of the power to function openly in the final artifact, and surfaces it as something that should have been brought to consciousness and partnership dialogue earlier.
