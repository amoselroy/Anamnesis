WORLD PATTERN 2026-06-22 — Regulatory data source richness varies dramatically by agency — 2026-06-22

PRINCIPLE: External data sources differ not just in format but in semantic richness; EMA's explicit status vocabulary outperforms FDA's binary approval flag and ANVISA's active/inactive catch-all for the same monitoring goal.

NARRATIVE: All three Tier 1 agencies (FDA, EMA, ANVISA) were confirmed to have CSV exports, but inspection revealed dramatic differences in information density. EMA provides explicit status vocabulary (Authorised, Withdrawn, Revoked, Suspended, Expired, Lapsed, Refused) with separate approval and withdrawal date columns. FDA's MarketingStatus just says "Approved" or "Discontinued" with no distinction between voluntary withdrawal and revocation. ANVISA's SITUACAO_REGISTRO only has "Ativo" or "Inativo", a catch-all that conflates expiration, withdrawal, and company discontinuation. Despite all three being "usable" sources, EMA's richness is clearly superior for tracking approval history and regulatory changes. The insight generalizes: when selecting external data sources, don't assume data availability is sufficient — evaluate the semantic richness and precision of the fields you need, as that determines the quality of downstream analysis.
