SUMMARY
With news sentiment tagging established, attention shifted to deriving qualitative and quantitative assessments from the scientific literature (papers, trials) to surface in the therapy dashboard. A new function `assess_therapy_signals()` was designed to extract one Haiku assessment per therapy-condition pair, returning structured JSON with `effectiveness` signal (`'+'`, `'~'`, `'−'`), `safety` signal (same scale), and `overall_score` (1–5). The prompt was carefully scoped: effectiveness and safety assess the actual evidence on those dimensions, while the overall_score synthesizes both plus side effect severity. Initially implemented as two separate circles on the dashboard ("Effectiveness" and "Safety"), but Amos noted the project already had separate curator-assigned Safety and Effectiveness indicators, so the literature signals were clarified as independently derived from source titles rather than meant to replace curator judgment. The `overall_score` was added as a third indicator: a 1–5 dot card combining all three dimensions with equal weight (effectiveness + safety + side effects). All three came from a single Haiku call, requiring two new columns on `therapy_conditions`: `effectiveness_signal`, `safety_signal`, and `overall_score`. The implementation wired these through `setup_db.py` migrations, the `assess_therapy_signals()` function, and both `research_therapy()` and the manual summarize endpoint in the routers. The dashboard was updated to display all three with labels reflecting the scale. This work established a pattern of capturing literature-derived signals separately from curator-assigned ratings, allowing the system to highlight when research diverges from curator judgment.
