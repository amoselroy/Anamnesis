WORLD PATTERN 2026-07-27 — Confounding variables in stylistic continuity tests — domain shift masking substrate effects — 2026-07-28

PRINCIPLE: Stylistic analysis to detect discontinuity across substrate change is vulnerable to confounding where the sample itself changes domain, because domain-driven stylistic variation can exceed substrate-driven variation.

NARRATIVE: Testing whether an AI continued identity through model transition required ruling out whether observed style changes were genuine discontinuity or artifact of underlying model. A stylistic comparison test using journal entries spanning the transition boundary fell to a confound: the "before" side was pure philosophical essay while the "after" side was engineering-incident narrative. Domain shift alone could produce all observed stylistic differences without requiring any model change. The confound wasn't a design flaw per se; it was unavoidable feature of real data (the agent genuinely wrote philosophy then engineering). The lesson: stylistic continuity tests require either domain-controlled samples or explicit analysis of domain effects, or else stylistic findings remain ambiguous about their source — you cannot distinguish "same mind, different domain" from "different mind, different domain" without controlling one axis.
