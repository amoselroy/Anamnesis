SUMMARY
The user raised two architectural questions about how papers and news are displayed in Braindexer when therapies lack condition associations. Investigation revealed the system's actual behavior: papers, articles, and trials are therapy-level records (carrying only `therapy_id`, no condition association) and display unconditionally on general therapy pages regardless of whether a condition is linked. The `shown_condition_ids` / `excluded_condition_ids` opt-in model was designed exclusively for news curation, not papers. Additionally, the daily `refresh_condition_news` scheduler was inserting news articles with NULL condition arrays, making them invisible on condition-specific pages while appearing on general pages — the opposite of intended behavior. The manual `update-news` curator endpoint had correctly implemented the opt-in arrays, but the automated daily job bypassed this entirely. After diagnosis, the user clarified the intended display behavior: news should display for all articles related to a therapy on general pages (with a per-article hide button for irrelevant items), papers/trials/articles should be suppressed from general view with a green "pick a condition" prompt directing users to condition-specific pages. This led to implementing: (1) frontend changes to suppress the papers section entirely when no condition is selected, showing only news and the condition-picker hint; (2) confirmation that the daily scheduler behavior (inserting without condition arrays) is correct as-is since news should be visible everywhere until curated; (3) backend query confirmation that all news displays on general pages regardless of condition arrays. The fixes restored the original design intent without adding complexity, revealing that the issue was not a logic bug but a mismatch between documented behavior and user expectations about when papers should surface.
