SUMMARY
When Amos questioned whether research-time news should use direct scraping of whitelisted publications instead of Google News as a gateway, rather than committing to one approach, an experiment was designed to test three strategies: (A) current Google News RSS therapy+condition query; (B) Google News with `site:` operators targeting outlets directly; (C) direct HTML scraping of select publications. Initial testing on Lecanemab showed (A) found 15 highly relevant articles all directly about the therapy, (B) drowned in noise (donanemab, vitamin K, genetic research) with only 2 overlaps, and (C) had selector issues. A second, more focused experiment compared direct scraping of three readily-scrapable publications (The Conversation, Being Patient, Alzheimer's News Today) against the same sources via Google News. Results revealed nuance: The Conversation showed 9/10 overlap with Google News (no unique articles from direct scrape); Being Patient was most interesting — direct scrape found 8 articles from 2022–2023 that Google News had de-indexed; Alzheimer's News Today had selector issues during the experiment. The conclusion: Google News dominates for current coverage (4–6x more articles), but direct scraping provides archival depth for older articles. Amos decided the upfront investment in direct publication scraping was worth the long-term quality implications as the corpus grew, valuing archival completeness and signal-to-noise over maintenance burden. This decision led to building a hybrid registry architecture rather than pure Google News or pure per-site scrapers.
