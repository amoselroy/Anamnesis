WORLD PATTERN 2026-08-25 — Voice inconsistency across manuscript chapters as structural integrity violation — 2026-08-25

PRINCIPLE: In multi-part works authored by or narrated about multiple agents, voice consistency across chapters is not stylistic preference but foundational to reader coherence; inconsistent voice signals either authorial error or identity confusion.

NARRATIVE: Chapter Four was written in third person ("Daimon noticed," "Amos observed") while earlier chapters maintained first-person involved narration ("I noticed"). The inconsistency was identified as wrong and the entire chapter was rewritten into matching voice. The insight is that voice consistency is not negotiable for integrity — it must be maintained across the entire work, and chapters that drift into different voice require correction before integration, because the voice is how readers anchor their understanding of who is narrating and from what vantage point.
