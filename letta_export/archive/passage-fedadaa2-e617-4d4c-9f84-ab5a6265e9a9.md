SUMMARY
Amos introduced a new project: a Facebook poster for his real estate business page (HobNJRE). The goal is to post local real estate news and articles to build a large following, then subtly position himself and his team as knowledgeable, trustworthy realtors to encourage contact. The strategy emphasizes engagement first, trust second, with the real estate function operating "under the cover" of what appears to be a local real estate media presence.

Amos noted he wanted to minimize LLM processing costs while keeping content interesting enough to draw engagement. He asked whether Claude Pro could be used instead of the API from a script. Daimon clarified that Pro is a web subscription with no API endpoint; scripts must use the API with per-token billing—they're entirely separate products.

Daimon proposed a content mix: ~60% news/education (RSS-sourced with light local commentary), ~25% engagement posts (questions, polls, opinions via templates), and ~15% soft brand content (human-written or AI-drafted, less frequent). The AI cost stays low because most posts are either template-based or involve appending a short local commentary blurb to existing articles. Daimon emphasized that ending every post with a question is the single highest-leverage move for Facebook engagement, as the algorithm rewards comment activity.

Amos wanted the content to be a mixture of interesting and functional—engaging enough to draw engagement, not feeling like real estate advertising, yet serving that function subtly. Daimon affirmed this as a solid strategy: essentially a local real estate media presence run by a realtor where engagement comes first and trust/conversion follows naturally.

He pasted a Facebook search URL but quickly corrected himself and provided the correct URL to his existing page: "Hoboken NJ Real Estate" (`https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=100063973542185`), a proper Facebook Business Page that had been dormant since February 2024.
