SUMMARY
Discussion surfaced tension between two timing strategies: waiting for CAB responses before fiscal sponsor outreach versus being proactive about platform quality. User decided to conduct a pre-flight clinical-audience review of the Braindexer platform itself, reasoning that when CAB members respond and potentially request to see the platform, it should be in a state credible enough that a skeptical researcher would see the vision clearly. Identified concerns included: (1) content breadth — the platform was described in outreach letters as "a handful of therapies, for demo purposes," which skeptical researchers might categorize as a prototype rather than a platform; (2) pending UI elements — discontinued therapy banners, FDA/EMA agency badges, and approved therapy indicators were designed but deployment status unclear; (3) summary quality — AI-generated summaries are the core value proposition and must appear substantively complete and accurate when reviewed by domain experts; (4) data quality signals — missing if the platform lacks visual indicators of curated, quality-controlled content. The decision was to "bite the bullet" and review the platform now through a clinician's eyes, identify gaps, and fix the most visible issues before any CAB member sees it. This positions the platform favorably for conversion when responses arrive, rather than scrambling to polish it reactively.
