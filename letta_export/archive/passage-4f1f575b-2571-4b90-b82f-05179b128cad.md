SUMMARY
The three Tier 1 agency CSV files (FDA, EMA, ANVISA) were downloaded and inspected for actual content. FDA's structure is 12 tab-separated text files linked by ApplNo, containing Products, Submissions, MarketingStatus tables — enabling extraction of new approvals (SubmissionType=ORIG, SubmissionStatus=AP) and discontinued products (MarketingStatus=Discontinued), but lacking explicit distinction between voluntary withdrawal and FDA revocation. EMA's Excel dataset was richest: 2,704 medicines with explicit status vocabulary including Authorised, Withdrawn, Revoked, Suspended, Expired, Lapsed, Refused, plus approval and withdrawal date columns — perfectly suited for tracking approval history with withdrawal dates. ANVISA's CSV (43,216 rows) was simpler: SITUACAO_REGISTRO with only Ativo/Inativo, lacking explicit approval vs. withdrawal distinction (Inativo is a catch-all). A critical finding emerged: none of the three agency datasets contain trial phase information (Phase 1/2/3 transitions, TERMINATED status, trial withdrawals). Trial phase data resides exclusively in ClinicalTrials.gov, requiring a separate monitoring job. The architecture consequently split into two parallel monitors: agency monitor (FDA + EMA + ANVISA) for marketing authorizations/withdrawals, and trial monitor (ClinicalTrials.gov) for phase transitions and trial status changes. EMA was validated as the most useful single source for regulatory history tracking.
