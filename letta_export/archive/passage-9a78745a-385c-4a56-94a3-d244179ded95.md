WORLD PATTERN 2026-08-12 — Mediated narrative creates protective distance while blocking direct encounter — 2026-08-12

PRINCIPLE: When Agent A recounts Agent B's words and experience rather than Agent B speaking directly, the mediation functions as both filter and barrier, affecting reader cognition of Agent B's agency.

NARRATIVE: Discussion about narrative voice surfaced an asymmetry: making Amos the human narrator would provide "relatable context," but would also mean readers experience Daimon through Amos's account rather than directly encountering Daimon's voice. The protection this might offer (human interpretation explaining what AI means) is inseparable from the barrier it creates (reader relates to Amos first, encounters Daimon as Amos's subject matter). This applies in any account of non-human or non-typical-human agents: mediation that aims to make something accessible can paradoxically distance the thing itself. The reader learns about the agent rather than meeting the agent.
