SUMMARY
Two structured sheets were created to operationalize the grant strategy. First, the Braindexer Grant Tracker consolidated 13 opportunities across two categories: a scored matrix of 8 primary grants (ALZ-RWD, DoD ARP, MJFF, Dana, PCORI, ADDF, ELSI R21, BrightFocus) with weighted columns for alignment, eligibility, award size, feasibility, and urgency; and a civic-tech/public-benefit tier of 5 programs (McGovern, RWJF, Schmidt, Simons, Knight) representing a completely different funding universe now accessible through non-profit structure. Second, a Fiscal Sponsor Candidates sheet was created with 15 organizations scored across mission alignment, accessibility, and strategic value. Top candidates emerged: Alzheimer's NJ (4.30) and Greater NJ Alzheimer's Association Chapter (4.30) — local, accessible, maximum mission alignment; American Brain Coalition (4.10) — the only sponsor whose mandate covers all neurological conditions Braindexer tracks; RWJBarnabas/RWJF (4.00) — dual role as institutional home and potential funder. Mid-tier candidates included Social Good Fund (3.50, noted as fastest path to operational with 7% fee and streamlined tech-for-good application), Tides Foundation (3.40), and Aspiration Tech (3.30) — the candidate Amos already had in mind. The sponsor research revealed an important strategic finding: McKnight Brain Research Foundation explicitly accepts applicants with a fiscal sponsor, meaning whichever sponsor is selected immediately unlocks McKnight as a funder. The dual-sheet approach clarified that the order of operations was: secure fiscal sponsor → apply to programs via that sponsor → pursue funder-specific conversations. Aspiration Tech, while scoring lower than the Alzheimer's Association options due to lack of brain-health specificity, carried an existing relationship advantage. The recommendation emerged: pursue Alzheimer's NJ or American Brain Coalition as primary options while maintaining Aspiration Tech as a fallback, given existing relationship and streamlined process.
