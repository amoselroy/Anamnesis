SUMMARY
The user correctly identified that per-therapy inline status checks during the research loop was inefficient. Instead, a bulk monthly/biweekly monitoring job that queries each regulatory agency once and diffs against prior snapshots is architecturally superior: it reduces API calls from 3×N to 3, catches new therapies not yet in the catalog, cleanly separates concerns (research loop handles papers/news; dedicated monitor handles regulatory state), and appropriately matches the monthly cadence of regulatory updates. A critical distinction emerged: discontinued therapies (trials halted, never approved) is fundamentally different from withdrawn therapies (was approved, then revoked or market-pulled), requiring two separate status values in the constraint. The approved_by JSONB structure was enhanced to include `withdrawn_year` to preserve historical approval records while signaling current unavailability. Regarding dismissed suggestions, the user's insight that the job can store last_checked_at per agency and only surface new changes eliminated the need for complex dismissal tracking — the same event won't resurface unless conditions change. However, the optional reversal point (curator dismisses then later wants to reconsider) was deemed worth supporting by storing dismissal history and allowing un-dismissal.
