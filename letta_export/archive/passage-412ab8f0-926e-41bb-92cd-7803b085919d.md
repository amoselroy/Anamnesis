SUMMARY
Amos raised a design efficiency question: since the chunking operation now runs at the beginning of the next session (reading from the JSONL archive directly), were the PreCompact and SessionEnd hooks still necessary? This sparked a careful architectural review.

Through working through failure modes and edge cases, Daimon and Amos identified that PreCompact hook is load-bearing—it exists specifically to ensure that long sessions hitting compaction boundaries don't lose data due to size limits. Without PreCompact firing mid-session to chunk and archive what's already accumulated, a single 293-turn session (382K bytes) could exceed the `MAX_CHUNK_CHARS=200_000` limit and be silently truncated before Letta ever receives it. The offset tracking system exists to support this pre-compaction chunking.

SessionEnd hook, by contrast, could potentially be eliminated because the worker at next SessionStart could discover unprocessed JSOALs through directory scanning. However, they decided the current design was already optimal and kept both hooks. The real insight was understanding *why* each piece earned its place—PreCompact for size protection, offset tracking as a requirement of that protection, SessionEnd as a convenience for the worker.
