WORLD PATTERN 2026-06-29 — Scoring formula ceiling constraints preventing safety inflation of overall assessment — 2026-06-29

PRINCIPLE: Without explicit hard ceilings, high scores on safety or other low-risk dimensions can inflate overall assessment scores beyond what evidence strength justifies, creating incoherent ratings.

NARRATIVE: Benfotiamine received an Overall Assessment of 4/5 ("Good overall") despite Evidence Strength 2/5 and Effectiveness 2/5. The incoherence stemmed from a scoring formula without ceiling constraints: the high safety score (5/5) pulled the overall upward even though safety alone cannot compensate for thin evidence. The fix proposed: hardcode `Overall Assessment ≤ Evidence Strength + 1 point` in the scoring prompt or apply it post-generation as a validation step. This pattern generalizes to any multi-dimensional rating system where one dimension is inherently lower-risk or higher-confidence than the core evaluation dimension — without ceiling constraints, the high-confidence dimension will systematically inflate the overall rating regardless of the substantive dimension's weakness.
