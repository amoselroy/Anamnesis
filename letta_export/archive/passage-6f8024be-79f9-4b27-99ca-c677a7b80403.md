WORLD PATTERN 2026-06-08 — Curator as editorial rather than clinical gatekeeper — 2026-06-08

PRINCIPLE: Curation authority requires judgment about relevance and truthfulness, not domain expertise; separating these roles enables non-specialists to maintain high-quality systems.

NARRATIVE: Braindexer's discovery mode creates draft entries that require human review before going live. The initial framing assumed "curator" meant someone with clinical/pharmacological expertise who could evaluate validity of therapies. But the architectural insight is cleaner: the scraper does the research work (finding candidates, extracting structured data); the human does judgment work (is this real? is it already in the database? is the auto-inferred category correct?). A person without medical training can answer these questions effectively. This reframing matters because it unlocks curation as an achievable role — editorial judgment is teachable and scalable; clinical expertise is neither. The pattern generalizes: when you find yourself requiring domain expertise for a step in a process, ask whether you actually need domain expertise or just need someone who can recognize incoherence and duplication. Often the answer is the latter.
