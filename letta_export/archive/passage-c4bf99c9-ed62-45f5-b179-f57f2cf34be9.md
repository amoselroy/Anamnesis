SUMMARY
With the revelation that Braindexer would operate as a non-profit (initially under a fiscal sponsor, eventually as a standalone 501(c)(3)), the entire grant priority matrix required rebuilding. The NSF SBIR, NIA SBIR, and NINDS SBIR programs — previously the top three opportunities — became completely ineligible. This was a critical strategic pivot because it eliminated $305K–$700K Phase I awards that would have been the fastest funding paths for a for-profit startup. However, it opened entirely different funding categories designed specifically for non-profits and civic-tech projects. The revised scoring framework (using the same weighted dimensions: alignment 30%, eligibility 25%, award size 20%, feasibility 15%, urgency 10%) produced a new tier 1 led by ALZ-RWD (Alzheimer's Association, 3.65 score), followed by DoD ARP Transforming Care (3.30) and Michael J. Fox Foundation (3.30). A second tier of civic-tech/public-benefit foundations emerged: Patrick J. McGovern Foundation, Robert Wood Johnson Foundation, Schmidt Futures, Simons Foundation, and Knight Foundation. These programs, sourced through web research rather than SPIN, were specifically aligned with AI-for-public-good and health equity framings. The restructuring revealed that fiscal sponsor selection would be strategically critical — different sponsors opened different funding pathways (for example, American Brain Coalition or Alzheimer's Association as sponsors would increase alignment scores with brain-health-specific funders and enable direct application to ALZ-RWD). The strategic insight: fiscal sponsor choice was not merely administrative but constitutive of funding eligibility and competitive positioning.
