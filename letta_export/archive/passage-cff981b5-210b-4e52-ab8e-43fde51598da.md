SUMMARY
Recognizing that Braindexer would be pursuing multiple funders simultaneously with different priorities and requirements, a master grant application narrative base was created as a Google Doc. The document was structured with five operational sections: (1) LOI template — ready to send immediately, with [SWAP] paragraphs that change per funder; (2) Extended narrative for full proposals — containing all standard grant sections (problem statement, solution, impact, sustainability); (3) Funder-specific swap paragraphs for six primary targets (Alzheimer's Association, DoD ARP, MJFF, RWJF, Knight Foundation, McGovern) — each emphasizing different aspects of Braindexer's value; (4) Copy-paste boilerplate — one-liner description, 50-word elevator pitch, problem statistics, platform statistics; (5) Budget framework with scale guidance per funder type and framing rules for every line item in patient/caregiver impact terms. The narrative choice — prioritizing patient and caregiver empowerment as the primary framing — was validated as resonant across every funder type and connective to equity, AI, and research access themes. The template was designed for efficiency: Amos would need to fill in only mission-critical variables (full name and background, current platform stats, fiscal sponsor details, requested dollar amounts) before customizing for each funder. The approach anticipated 6–8 simultaneous applications across different opportunity timelines (ALZ-RWD 2027 cycle; DoD Sep 24; Knight/McGovern rolling LOIs; RWJF and others with varying deadlines). The master template solved the operational problem of avoiding duplicative writing effort while maintaining funder-specific customization.
