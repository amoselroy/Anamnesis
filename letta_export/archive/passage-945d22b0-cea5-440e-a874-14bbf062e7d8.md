SUMMARY
After research on Gamma Sensory Stimulation completed successfully, the results included papers that were clearly off-topic (e.g., "Non-targeted metabolomic profiling of Cistus species," "Early aperiodic EEG changes," "Therapeutic potential of caudatin") despite the new quoted PubMed search and abstract-aware ranking. Investigation revealed these were remnants from prior research runs using unquoted keyword searches that matched too broadly (e.g., "stimulation" matched TMS, "gamma" matched gamma-secretase inhibitors, etc.). The papers were permanently in the `sources` table but were not re-found by the new, more precise search. Rather than incrementally fix individual papers, Amos decided to flush all scientific papers (`source_type IN ('paper', 'article', 'trial_registration')`) and restart research from scratch, since only 6 therapies were active. This was cleanest: 133 rows deleted, keeping news articles untouched (those were manually curated per condition). The cleanup required using the Neon SQL console directly because the local development database connection attempt hit a wrong PostgreSQL instance. After flushing, Gamma Sensory Stimulation research was re-run and pulled only high-quality, directly relevant papers. This revealed an architectural lesson: legacy search implementations leave permanent cruft in the database; when search precision improves dramatically (unquoted → quoted), a one-time flush + fresh start is often cleaner than trying to identify and remove only the bad rows.
