WORLD PATTERN 2026-06-19 — Opt-in visibility model as design philosophy — 2026-06-19

PRINCIPLE: Opt-in (nothing visible by default) vs. opt-out (everything visible by default) is a fundamental stance toward what work the curator must do, and changes the scaling behavior when new conditions are added.

NARRATIVE: During the design of condition-scoped news visibility, an initial opt-out model (articles visible everywhere, curators hide from specific conditions) seemed simpler. However, Amos identified a critical scaling problem: adding a new condition tomorrow would instantly flood that condition's view with hundreds of articles never intended for it. The curator burden becomes immediate and massive. The opt-in alternative (articles invisible everywhere, curators explicitly include for specific conditions) flips the burden: adding a new condition creates a clean slate; curators build each condition's pool at their own pace. The philosophical difference: opt-out treats the system as chaotic by default (curator work is suppression); opt-in treats it as silent by default (curator work is illumination). In contexts where conditions/dimensions grow over time or where cross-dimensional relevance varies, opt-in prevents the scaling cliff that opt-out creates. This principle generalizes to any multi-dimensional curation system where new dimensions are added incrementally.
