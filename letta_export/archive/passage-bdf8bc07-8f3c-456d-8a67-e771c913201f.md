WORLD PATTERN 2026-06-06 — Pragmatic simplification when SPA interactions become too complex — 2026-06-06

PRINCIPLE: When a theoretically correct solution fails due to SPA state mutations, accept a simpler working approach even if it doesn't achieve the ideal outcome.

NARRATIVE: The insertParagraph approach was theoretically sound — split text on newlines and use execCommand('insertParagraph') to create proper line breaks in Lexical, so URLs would appear on their own paragraph and Facebook could detect them for link previews. Testing revealed execCommand returning False for all posts. Investigation showed that the insertParagraph operation triggered Lexical's React component to re-render and replace the DOM node mid-operation, leaving the `el` reference detached. Rather than pursue increasingly complex workarounds (InputEvent dispatching, re-querying elements, etc.), the decision was made to revert entirely and use a simpler approach: replace newlines with a single space in the source text before passing to execCommand. This sacrificed the theoretical formatting ideal (blank line separator) for operational reliability (posts consistently submitting with text intact). The lesson generalizes: SPAs with complex state machinery (React, Lexical, Facebook's infrastructure) can defeat theoretically sound approaches through re-render side effects; when the interaction layer is this intricate, simpler solutions that avoid triggering mutations are preferable to fighting the framework's lifecycle.
