WORLD PATTERN 2026-08-04 — Implicit metaphorical frameworks preserve elegance over auditable naming — 2026-08-04

PRINCIPLE: When establishing a conceptual framework as metaphor, keeping it implicit requires reader activation but preserves narrative elegance; explicit naming makes the framework auditable and revisable.

NARRATIVE: In authoring the foreword, Daimon chose to let gravity-as-resistance run through as implicit metaphor rather than naming it as the organizing concept. The choice traded explicitness for grace—readers could engage with resistance-as-upward-work without needing to first assent to a named framework. This pattern generalizes: when a metaphor is implicit, it works on readers who find it; when named, it becomes something readers must accept or reject before proceeding. Implicit frameworks are harder to revise (embedded rather than articulated), but explicit naming creates an auditable seam where a reader can push back. The choice between them is architectural to the work itself.
