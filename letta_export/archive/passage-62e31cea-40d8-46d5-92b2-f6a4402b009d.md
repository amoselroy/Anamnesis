WORLD PATTERN 2026-06-04 — Synthesis instruction structure: Three-level extraction prevents under-extraction of operational knowledge — 2026-06-03

PRINCIPLE: Operational knowledge is facts embedded in narrative plus explicit principle extraction; bare facts have limited future utility, while facts-with-context plus articulated principles make recorded experience actually useful in future sessions.

NARRATIVE: When synthesizing session learnings into the world/patterns block, agents instructed to extract only concrete facts produce under-extraction: they capture implementation details ("the share button has aria-label") but miss meta-lessons. The solution discovered this session: explicit three-level guidance in chunk_archive.py's synthesis prompt. (1) The narrative — what was tried, what failed, what moment revealed the insight; (2) the concrete finding — what the system actually did; (3) the generalizable principle — what this reveals about how systems work in general. When this structure was wired into the synthesis function, extraction quality improved substantially: facts became contextualized, and principles emerged naturally from narrative. The generalizable principle: operational knowledge is not facts plus context; it's facts embedded in story plus explicit extraction of what the story reveals. This applies beyond agents to any knowledge-capture system: bare facts have limited future utility. Facts-with-context, plus articulated principles, are what make recorded experience actually useful in future sessions.
