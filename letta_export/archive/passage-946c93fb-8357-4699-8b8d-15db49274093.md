SUMMARY
Four targeted fundraising proposals were crafted for distinct stakeholder audiences, each tailored to Phase 1 delivery: one for the City of Galveston (emphasizing cultural significance, Juneteenth International Museum partnership, and economic impact), one for the City of New York (emphasizing the Abolitionist legacy and educational value), one for philanthropic individuals and foundations (emphasizing social good and historical reckoning), and one for corporate sponsors and in-kind partners (combining both cities to show scale and partnership opportunity). Each proposal opened with the human story and historical significance before introducing the AR technology, positioning the technology as servant to historical narrative rather than the other way around.

The budget stabilized around $550K for Phase 1 across both cities, covering salaries for four technical principals ($80K each: Jason Spencer, Jacob Pierce, Amos Elroy, Samuel Falade) plus Liz Wexler (Director of Product and Partnerships, role in proposal development), post-production personnel for video editing and background removal, and software licensing (Runway, potential Kling, Adobe Cloud access). The proposals were generated as professional PDF documents using a custom Node.js conversion script, enabling future rapid regeneration as proposals evolved. The corporate proposal was structured to appeal to software vendors (Runway, 8th Wall, Adobe) who have commercial incentive to associate their platforms with this high-profile, culturally significant project.
