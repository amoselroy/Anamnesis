SUMMARY
A candid assessment was provided of the actual probability of success for different grant opportunities, organized by likelihood and timeline. Most accessible grants (Phase 1, 25–35% success probability per application): NJ-based community foundations (NJ Health Foundation, Community Foundation of NJ) benefit from geographic preference for local initiatives; McKnight Brain Research Foundation explicitly accepts fiscally sponsored projects in the $75K–$150K range; Knight Foundation accepts rolling LOIs for civic tech/public benefit initiatives. Moderate probability (Phase 2, 15–25%, requires CAB and fiscal sponsor): Dana Foundation, MJFF, regional foundations (Horizon BCBS of NJ), McGovern Foundation. These have seen numerous health democratization pitches; the differentiator is Braindexer's three-pillar model (research catalog + lifestyle interventions + trial recruitment bridge) combined with a named clinical advisory board. Competitive tier (Phase 3, 5–15%, requires 501(c)(3) + academic partner + operational track record): DoD ARP, ALZ-RWD, PCORI, RWJF. These are not impossible but weight organizational track record heavily in review. The DoD ARP Sep 24 deadline is likely too aggressive without an academic partner ready to co-apply. The highest single-leverage action: securing one named clinical advisor on the CAB moves every probability estimate up 5–10 points by reframing the initiative as clinically credible rather than developer-driven. Realistic first-year scenario: 8–10 applications submitted, 2–3 acceptances, total funding $100K–$250K. This constitutes a strong first year for early-stage health non-profit and establishes the organizational track record that significantly strengthens Year 2 applications. NJ-specific funders should be prioritized as Phase 1 targets because geographic motivation to fund local initiatives is a concrete advantage unavailable nationally.
