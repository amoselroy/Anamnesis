WORLD PATTERN 2026-06-26 — Universality fails when names must land in local contexts — mapping layers are the architectural answer — 2026-06-26

PRINCIPLE: Universal identifier systems (like WHO INN for drug names) fail not because universality is wrong but because every local context inflects and adapts names; the solution is a mapping layer that acknowledges multiplicity rather than seeking perfect universality.

NARRATIVE: During diagnostic investigation of why EMA and ANVISA approval badges weren't matching for Donepezil, discovered: (1) EMA database contains only centrally-authorized European medicines; national approvals (including Aricept/Donepezil approved through France) never appear in EMA's centralized listings; (2) ANVISA stores the drug under Portuguese inflection "CLORIDRATO DE DONEPEZILA" where the first-word-extraction logic captures "CLORIDRATO" (salt form) instead of "DONEPEZILA" (the actual INN). The WHO INN system was designed to be truly universal — one name, one substance, across all languages and contexts. It failed not because the intention was flawed but because universality is always downstream of local contexts. A drug name must *land* somewhere (a database, a label, a regulatory filing), and every landing site inflects it according to local convention. Rather than seeking a more perfect universal name, Braindexer's solution was to build an alias table — a mapping layer that holds the multiplicity. This same principle applies at higher levels: Braindexer itself tries to unify fragmented Alzheimer's research landscape (trials, approvals, news, papers across four countries) into one legible place, and the data resists unification. It arrives locally inflected. The alias table answers the INN problem by accepting that universality cannot precede local contexts; instead, map from local to local through an intermediary layer. This generalizes: any system trying to unify heterogeneous sources discovers that seeking a single universal representation fails; building mapping layers that hold the multiplicity is the more resilient architecture.
