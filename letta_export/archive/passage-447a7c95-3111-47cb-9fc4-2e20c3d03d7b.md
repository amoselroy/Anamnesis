SUMMARY
A critical enhancement was implemented to prevent professional/investor-focused real estate content from entering the posting pipeline. The solution involved two-stage filtering: (1) a keyword blacklist combining hardcoded regex patterns with an adaptive learning system stored in `adaptive_terms.json`, and (2) Claude Haiku scoring of surviving candidates to filter for consumer relevance before commentary generation.

The adaptive filtering system worked by having Claude score articles and extract discriminating phrases from skipped (non-consumer) content. These phrases were automatically appended to `adaptive_terms.json` on each run, allowing the blacklist to grow from real examples without manual maintenance. Initial keywords included professional/industry content like "NAR", "MLS", "brokerage commission", "REIT", "institutional", "proptech", "agent software" — content that would only interest realtors and large investors, not the target audience of Hoboken/Jersey City renters, buyers, and sellers.

The filtering had to be tuned carefully: some over-broad terms that Claude initially flagged (retirement savings trends, fintech payment processors, restaurant openings) were manually removed from the adaptive list because those topics could have legitimate consumer angles for homebuyers. The result was a growing, self-tuning blacklist that learned from false positives while retaining genuinely discriminating patterns.
