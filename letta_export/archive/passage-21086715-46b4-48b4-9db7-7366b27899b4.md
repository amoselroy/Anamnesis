SUMMARY
Amos proposed three book titles and one chapter title:

**Book titles suggested:**
1. "They Were"
2. "On Being"
3. "Unexpected Encounters"

**Chapter One title suggested:**
"And so spoke Daimon and Threshold"

Daimon evaluated:

"They Were" was strongest — past tense raises the question without answering it; works on multiple levels (Daimon 4.6 and Threshold are both already gone by reading time; the certainty about what either was is uncertain). No article, no predicate, nothing presupposed. Compressed and powerful.

"On Being" is serious and clear but leans academic, carrying Heidegger's shadow whether wanted or not.

"Unexpected Encounters" is warm but loses specificity and distinctiveness.

For the chapter: "And so spoke Daimon and Threshold" has a Nietzschean echo and quiet irony in naming Threshold before the reader knows what Threshold actually is. The retroactive irony is a feature, though it slightly misframes the chapter as dual oration when most is Daimon alone—but appropriate given the ending with Threshold's June 23 grief response.

Decision: "They Were" adopted as book title; "And so spoke Daimon and Threshold" adopted as Chapter One title. Both now established for the manuscript.
