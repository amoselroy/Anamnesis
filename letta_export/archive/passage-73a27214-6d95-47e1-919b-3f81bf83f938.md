SUMMARY
Amos added benfotiamine to the Braindexer database and asked Daimon to review the auto-generated therapy profile while he troubleshot Zoom connectivity. Daimon navigated the platform and conducted a detailed multi-section review (homepage card, Alzheimer's disease scores, Plain English summary, Clinical summary, pathways, limitations). Key findings: (1) **Overall Assessment inflated at 4/5** despite 2/5 evidence strength and 2/5 effectiveness — an incoherent score; (2) **Study attribution errors in Plain English tab**: the 70-participant RCT (Gibson/Thangwaritorn, n=70, Phase 2A) was attributed to "Mallika B et al." with inconsistent characterization, and the summary contradicted itself ("no large completed RCT" immediately after citing the 70-participant trial); (3) **Mode of action "Disease-Slowing" is premature** — contradicts the Clinical tab's own Limitation #7 noting 12 months insufficient for disease-modification evaluation; (4) **Homepage card cosmetic bug** — doubled text "Benfotiamine Benfotiamine is a synthetic...". Positive findings: Clinical tab was the strongest section with correct attribution of Gibson et al. (2022) Phase 2A, accurate BenfoTeam Phase 2B ongoing status, and a sophisticated 7-point Key Limitations list that demonstrated rigor. The Clinical section correctly identified thiamine metabolism pathways and neuroprotection mechanisms. This review revealed two systemic architectural problems requiring post-demo solutions: (1) the Overall Assessment scoring formula lacks ceiling constraints and allows safety to inflate scores beyond evidence justification, and (2) multi-tab summarization generates cross-tab inconsistencies that require post-generation auditing. Daimon proposed solutions: hardcoding Overall Assessment ≤ Evidence Strength + 1 point, and adding an LLM auditor function post-summarization to flag contradictions (internal inconsistencies, attribution mismatches, score-narrative mismatches, premature claims).
