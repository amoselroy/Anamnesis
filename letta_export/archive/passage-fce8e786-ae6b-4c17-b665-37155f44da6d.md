SUMMARY
Two complementary expert oversight structures were identified as critical to Braindexer's credibility and long-term platform viability. Near-term, a Clinical Advisory Board (CAB) of 3–5 credentialed experts provides the trustworthiness and regulatory/clinical credibility essential for health-focused grant funding. Standard CAB composition includes a neurologist or geriatrician with brain health specialty, a patient advocate or caregiver, ideally a retired FDA/EMA reviewer for regulatory credibility, and a bioethicist. Recruitment paths identified: AlzForum's editorial network, Rutgers/NJMS neurology faculty, RWJBarnabas Memory Center (dual role as fiscal sponsor candidate), AAN member directory. Budget: $2K–$8K/year in stipends, already incorporated into the budget planner. Long-term, the project can evolve toward a Verified Professional Contributor Network — a Cochrane or Scholarpedia model where AI-generated syntheses are validated and extended by verified professionals. Infrastructure requirements include identity verification (ORCID for researchers, NPI numbers for US physicians), trust tiers (unverified read-only → verified professionals → trusted contributors → editorial review board), and attribution (contributors credited on entries they validate, creating academic incentive alignment). This professional contributor model opened three new grant channels: PCORI (patient and stakeholder engagement is a core requirement, and professional participation is the strongest answer); NIH Community Engaged Research (CEnR) programs (explicitly structured around professional community participation in health knowledge); and Wikimedia Foundation / open knowledge infrastructure funders (Braindexer as community-validated open health knowledge fits their mission). Strategic importance: the moment a named credentialed expert joins the advisory board, the project shifts from "developer-built tech tool" to "credible clinical initiative" — this single change moves grant probability estimates up by 5–10 points across all tiers. Professional contributor networks also address a core PCORI requirement around stakeholder engagement while simultaneously serving the platform's quality and trustworthiness goals.
