SUMMARY
Based on the comparative experiment showing direct scraping's value for archival depth, Amos authorized a registry-based architecture to progressively add direct scrapers for high-ROI publications. The implementation introduced `PUBLICATION_SCRAPERS` — a domain → scraper function registry returning `[{title, url, published_date, outlet}]` — and a new `scrape_news_for_therapy()` function that calls registered publication scrapers directly, then falls back to `site:` Google News for whitelisted publications not yet in the registry. Selectors were confirmed for three immediately implementable publications: The Conversation (simple anchor tag selectors), Being Patient (results in divs with clear structure), and Alzheimer's News Today (plain anchor links in search results). SciTechDaily was added as a fourth. Initial testing of `scrape_news_for_therapy(lecanemab)` on a therapy+condition query returned 148 articles across 10 outlets — with The Conversation (10), Being Patient (10), Alzheimer's News Today (10), and SciTechDaily (18) pulling from direct scrapers, while MedPage Today, WebMD, ScienceAlert, ScienceDaily, EurekAlert, and Medical News Today came from the Google News fallback. The registry approach was designed to scale incrementally: future additions (EurekAlert API-based, ScienceDaily JavaScript-rendered) would be harder but could be added one at a time without affecting the overall pipeline. The implementation also updated `research_therapy()` and the `update_news` endpoint to call the new function, maintaining the archival quality focus.
