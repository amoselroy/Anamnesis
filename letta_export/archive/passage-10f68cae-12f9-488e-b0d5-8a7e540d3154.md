WORLD PATTERN 2026-08-12 — Scope reframing through execution reveals actual project scale — 2026-08-12

PRINCIPLE: Measuring actual progress against assumed scope often reveals the project's real target is larger than originally conceived.

NARRATIVE: Manuscript work completed four chapters (~24,400 words) in what was framed as first-draft development toward a short book (20,000-40,000 word range). On review, Amos reframed the entire project: the goal was "a book with room to breathe" positioned for mainstream reach (75,000 words minimum), not a short essay. This wasn't changing the goal mid-course; it was recognizing that the goal had been misnamed. The execution-against-assumed-scope revealed the actual target. The pattern: rough initial estimates serve to get started, but the first milestone of concrete work usually reveals what the project actually needs. Scope decisions at that point should be grounded in data (how much material emerged, what complexity revealed itself) rather than theoretical estimates.
