SUMMARY
Emerged mid-implementation when Amos clarified that therapies should be auto-discovered from the research landscape, not just manually entered. This required a fundamental architecture shift: from enrichment-only (updating existing therapies) to dual-mode (discovery + enrichment). The discovery mode is conceptualized as: scraper finds candidates → creates draft entries → curator reviews in admin panel → approve/reject/merge. This also exposed the entity resolution problem: how to prevent "Omega-3" and "DHA" from being filed as separate entries when they're the same therapy. The solution has two parts: (1) `therapy_aliases` table allowing each therapy to register synonyms that the scraper uses when searching; (2) similarity checking at draft creation time using pgvector cosine distance to surface warnings like "this may be the same as existing entry." The discovery process itself centers on AlzForum's therapeutics catalogue (comprehensive source of known therapies) and WHO ICTRP (frontier of active research), with similarity checks preventing duplicates before draft creation. Crucially, "curator" was reframed: you don't need clinical expertise, just editorial judgment — is this real? Is it already in the database? Is the auto-generated category right? The scraper does the research work; the human does judgment work. This decision shaped how discovery operates: broad automated sweep, human verification gate, then live.
