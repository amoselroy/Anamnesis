SUMMARY
During the Beeri meeting, she asked about Diranersen (Biogen's tau-targeting antisense oligonucleotide, BIIB080), which had just shown Phase 2 results at AAIC 2026 with up to 26% cognitive decline slowing at lower doses, advancing to Phase 3. When Amos tried adding it to Braindexer, the system found zero sources — a complete miss. Investigation revealed that Diranersen's trials exist on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05399888, the CELIA trial precursor, condition: "Mild Cognitive Impairment Due to Alzheimer's Disease" / "Alzheimer's Disease Dementia") and are queryable by drug name alone, but the scraper's specific query combining `condition="Alzheimer"` (bare word) with `intervention="diranersen"` returns zero results. Root cause: CT.gov's Essie search doesn't match bare "Alzheimer" against compound condition phrases like "...Due to Alzheimer's Disease" or "Alzheimer's Disease Dementia" — it requires exact or near-exact token matching. Testing showed `"Alzheimer's"` (with apostrophe) and `"Alzheimer Disease"` both matched fine, but bare `"Alzheimer"` did not. Daimon fixed this by normalizing the condition string inside `scrape_ctgov_trials()` specifically to use `"Alzheimer's Disease"` instead of the bare `"Alzheimer"` default, verified end-to-end against both Diranersen and Semaglutide (zero regression), and pushed the fix (commit `1abab52`). Once live, Diranersen's empty therapy entry was re-researched and populated with its trial correctly. This was a distinct failure mode from the earlier identified blind spots: not trials that don't self-tag AD at all, but trials that *do* self-tag AD-relevant conditions correctly and still get missed due to token-matching sensitivity in the query engine.
